05 / The Lab Method

Creative instinct. Scientific process.

I learned to work in research labs and science fairs: form a hypothesis, test it, measure, repeat. I bring that to your content.

  1. H

    Hypothesis

    We pick one story angle and one audience feeling, and write down why we think it'll land.

  2. V

    Variants

    Each concept is cut with 3 different hooks, so we learn what opens best instead of guessing.

  3. T

    Test

    Files labelled and organised (C04_H2_22s), ready to post or run as ads in minutes.

  4. I

    Iterate

    We read retention, saves and comments, then build the next round around what worked.

08 / Objections, handled

Questions brands ask before they book.

You're early in your career. Why should I trust you?

Fair question. I don't have a wall of agency case studies yet, and I won't pretend to. What I do have: a portfolio you can judge with your own eyes, a track record of building things that get recognised (national science awards, TV features, a brand that sold out twice), and a founding rate that makes the risk small. Start with one pilot video and judge the work.

Do you make UGC?

Not the scripted, spammy kind. I make creator-style content that feels native to the feed: real places, real moments, real use of the product. It's filmed and cut with a cinematic eye, so it looks premium without looking like an ad.

How fast do I get my first cuts?

48–72 hours from receiving footage and the brief. Need it for a launch tomorrow? Rush 24h delivery is available. Ask on the call.

What if I don't like the edit?

Every deliverable includes 2 revision rounds. Most edits land in one, because we agree on the story and hook before I touch the timeline.

Can you shoot too, or only edit?

Both. I can edit footage you send me, or shoot in and around Montréal myself. Cinematography is one of my favourite parts of the process.

Can you make French or Mandarin versions?

Yes. I'm fluent in English and French and speak Mandarin, so I can create native-feeling content for Québec, France and Chinese-speaking audiences, not just translated captions.

Do you help with ideas and strategy?

Yes. I'll suggest angles, hooks and formats based on what's working on the platforms right now, and plan shots before filming so the edit has what it needs.

Montréal ·

I never really set out to become a content creator.

I've always been someone who likes building things and trying new things. I've loved creating and making art since I was a kid. I used to draw a lot and make paintings. Then I fell in love with science for the innovation and discovery. I've done science projects, research, sports, school events, travelled with friends, and somehow always ended up starting another project or chasing another idea.

At some point, I realized I was experiencing a lot of things I would have loved to see someone else document: what it's like being a student in Montréal, figuring out what you want to do with your life, discovering new places, building projects with friends, trying new sports, doing scientific research, engaging in student congress, meeting new people, going to events, and saying yes to opportunities even when you have no idea where they'll lead.

So I started filming.

Not because I have my life figured out, but because I don't.

I think there's something really interesting about documenting that stage of life where you're ambitious, curious, slightly lost, and constantly trying new things. I call them my "side quests": little experiences that might eventually turn into something bigger.

That's what I want my content to be: an unfolding story rather than a perfectly curated life.

And for brands, I want to bring them into that story naturally. Whether it's a product I genuinely use, a place I'm discovering, an event I'm attending, or an experience I wouldn't normally get to have, I want the audience to feel like they're coming along for the experience rather than watching an advertisement.

I'm not trying to sell people a perfect life.

I'm trying to make the process of figuring it out worth watching.

— Keyu
