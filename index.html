01 / Selected work

Every frame has a job.

En Marge × Fondation Charles-Bruneau · Docuseries launch

Day 1 of raising $10K for kids with cancer: is it even possible?

  1. The feeling

    I wanted people to feel the weight of the challenge, and then want to help. Not pity, hope.

  2. The hook

    Me, alone on an empty stage, asking if a few students can fill 1,000 seats. The empty room says "impossible" before I do.

  3. The story

    Underdog first, then the why (kids with cancer), then the concrete goal ($10K), then a cliffhanger. Every beat answers "why should I care?"

  4. Music & sound

    Sparse and quiet in the empty theatre, then it swells when the packed-concert footage hits. The contrast does the talking.

  5. Type & look

    Clean documentary title cards and French/English subtitles, so nobody in Montréal is left out.

What your brand can take from it

People don't follow announcements, they follow journeys. Show where you're starting, admit what you don't know yet, and invite your audience to come along.

Frozen Heart · Shanghai night · Music edit

Kinetic typography locked to the city, beat by beat.

  1. The feeling

    That late-night, headphones-on feeling of walking through a city alone.

  2. The hook

    A street singer, then a whip pan into the night as the first words lock onto the screen on the beat. Sound and motion grab you before you know what it's about.

  3. The story

    Almost no plot, on purpose: the street, strange shops with moving heads, a quiet late-night meal, a rainy drive home. A mood arc instead of a narrative.

  4. Music & sound

    The song decided the edit. I cut on the heavy beats and let the quieter moments breathe so it plays like a music video.

  5. Type & look

    Words are 3D-tracked onto walls and storefronts so the text feels like part of the city, not stickers on top. Neon cyan, magenta and amber grade.

What your brand can take from it

When music, text and image move together, people rewatch. For fashion, music and nightlife brands, that's how a product becomes part of a world instead of an ad.

Aloya Festival · Montréal · Event recap

Turning sponsor activations into 30 seconds of pure FOMO.

  1. The feeling

    FOMO. I wanted someone on their couch to think "why am I not there right now?"

  2. The hook

    A golden-hour crowd shot with "POV: summer in Montréal", then me welcoming you in. Place, vibe and guide, all in under 3 seconds.

  3. The story

    I treated the festival like a game: small challenges with rewards (ring toss for free drinks, a sprint, a dunk), each one a level, with the sunset DJ set as the finale.

  4. Music & sound

    A driving pop beat with every jump cut on it, so the energy never drops below the crowd's.

  5. Type & look

    Pop-up text like a game score ("200 points", "Duuuunk") and a warm, saturated grade to make summer look as good as it felt.

What your brand can take from it

Sponsors don't need more logo placements, they need to be part of the fun. Show people playing with your activation and it becomes the reason they come back next year.

Chaoxiyuan · Shanghai · Immersive dining

A 9-course theatrical banquet, filmed like a VIP pass.

  1. The feeling

    Like getting a VIP pass to the dinner everyone online is talking about.

  2. The hook

    A fan snaps open, then straight to acrobats flying above the tables. Spectacle first, explanation after.

  3. The story

    Transformation (costume, hair, makeup), then the sets, then the 9-course banquet, then where it is. You experience it in the same order I did.

  4. Music & sound

    Traditional drums drive the edit, and I synced the cuts to the aerial dancers' choreography.

  5. Type & look

    Minimal text so the costumes and lighting carry it, whip transitions on every costume change, and a rich theatrical grade.

What your brand can take from it

For restaurants and experiences, let viewers live it from the guest's seat. When they can picture themselves there, they book.

03 / How I learn

I break down every video I make.

After every video, I study what worked and what didn't: the hook, the pacing, where people dropped off. Here's one example, beat by beat. On your project, I apply those lessons in whatever style fits your brand, from storytelling to clean product content.

  1. 0–10s

    Hook with proof

    "I may or may not have gotten free Fast Passes…" A risky claim, and the wristbands in hand right away, so the viewer has to see how.

  2. 10–22s

    A villain everyone shares

    $50 tickets, $80 Fast Passes, $10 water. One meme turns personal frustration into something every viewer relates to.

  3. 22–33s

    The lightbulb

    Endless lines, then the realization: people leaving the park throw away valid passes. The plan is set up in one beat.

  4. 33–53s

    Real tension, real footage

    Cold-approaching families at the exit, filmed handheld. Will it work? The viewer stays to find out.

  5. 53–73s

    Payoff & a reason to share

    Skipping the queues, bonus food passes, then a game-style stat screen (+4 Charisma) that ends it on a laugh.

04 / How we work together

Creative instinct. Scientific process.

Start with an idea, figure out how to make it work, test it, fail, change something, try again. I learned it in research labs. Now I use it on your content.

  1. R

    Background research

    I study your brand, your audience, your competitors and what's already working on the platforms, so we start from what's known.

  2. H

    Hypothesis

    A clear, testable bet: "If we open on X and tell it as Y, this audience will stay and act." Written down before anything is filmed.

  3. E

    Experiment

    Plan, shoot and edit. Each concept is cut with different hooks, changing one variable at a time so the test is fair.

  4. A

    Analyze

    Retention curves, watch time, saves, shares, comments. We look at what the numbers and the audience are actually saying.

  5. C

    Conclusion

    Did the evidence support the bet? I share what worked, what didn't and why, in plain language.

  6. I

    Iterate & improve

    The next round starts from the winners. Every video makes the next one smarter.

08 / Objections, handled

Questions brands ask before they book.

You're early in your career. Why should I trust you?

Fair question. I don't have a wall of agency case studies yet, and I won't pretend to. What I do have: a portfolio you can judge with your own eyes, festivals, venues and causes that already asked me to make content for them, a fashion brand I founded that sold out twice, and national awards for my research. Start with one pilot video and judge the work.

Do you make UGC?

Not the scripted, spammy kind. I make creator-style content that feels native to the feed: real places, real moments, real use of the product. It's filmed and cut with a cinematic eye, so it looks premium without looking like an ad.

How fast do I get my first cuts?

48–72 hours from receiving footage and the brief. Need it for a launch tomorrow? Rush 24h delivery is available. Ask on the call.

What if I don't like the edit?

Every deliverable includes 2 revision rounds. Most edits land in one, because we agree on the story and hook before I touch the timeline.

Can you shoot too, or only edit?

Both. I can edit footage you send me, or shoot in and around Montréal myself. Events, restaurants, launches: cinematography is one of my favourite parts of the process.

Can you make French or Mandarin versions?

Yes. I'm fluent in English and French and speak Mandarin, so I can create native-feeling content for Québec, France and Chinese-speaking audiences, not just translated captions.

Do you help with ideas and strategy?

Yes. I'll suggest angles, hooks and formats based on what's working on the platforms right now, and plan shots before filming so the edit has what it needs.

Montréal ·

I never really set out to become a content creator.

I've always just liked creating things.

When I was younger, it was drawings and paintings. Then I got into science and research. I loved that you could start with an idea, figure out how to make it work, test it, fail, change something, and try again.

That way of thinking ended up following me everywhere.

I started making science projects. I got involved in research. I played sports, organized events, travelled, joined student leadership organizations, and somehow always found another project to start.

Eventually, I started filming it.

At first, it was just because I wanted to remember these moments. But I also realized that I loved the process of turning something that happened in real life into a story that someone else would also want to watch and take inspiration from.

That became the videos I created.

A research project. A random trip with friends. Trying a new sport. Exploring Montréal. Going to an event. Building something from scratch.

I film the things I'm genuinely curious about, and I think that's why people connect with it. I'm not trying to look like I have life figured out. I'm a student trying things, making mistakes, meeting people, and trying to build something great to positively impact my environment.

Then something unexpected happened.

People started asking me to make videos for them.

Brands, businesses, events, restaurants and other creators started seeing the same thing I was doing for myself and asking, "We love your work! Can you do that for us?"

That's how this became more than just my personal content.

Today, I see what I do as sitting somewhere between a creator and a creative marketing studio.

On one side, I make content around my own life and build an audience that trusts my taste. On the other, I work directly with brands to turn their ideas, products and experiences into videos people actually want to watch.

And I think doing both makes me better at each.

When I'm making my own content, I learn what makes a real person stop scrolling, keep watching, and actually care.

When I'm working with a brand, I get to take that knowledge and apply it to their story.

I don't believe good branded content should feel like an advertisement with a creator standing in front of it.

It should feel like something you would have watched anyway.

That's the standard I try to bring to everything I make. I want the content to feel natural enough that my audience trusts it, but intentional enough that a brand knows why it is working.

So, ready to build something iconic that actually converts?
